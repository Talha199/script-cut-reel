# Front-load the hook, clear Instagram's UI

The script-cut-reel skill's editing core is already in line with 2025–2026 practice: script-driven take selection, waveform-based silence removal, the ASR audit and colour-only karaoke captions all hold up. What needs changing is mostly **where things appear on screen, when they appear, and a handful of technical defaults**. There are three structural problems. **Timeline order**: the hook line runs into a full-screen intro clip, and the title card arrives about 8–10 s in. That is after Instagram's 3-second Skip Rate window and after the ~8.5 s average Reel watch time. The topic needs to be on screen from frame 0, and the default should be no intro clip. **Placement**: the captions' bottom edge sits at y≈1400, inside the bottom 35% that Meta tells advertisers to keep clear. The title (y=210) and the progress line (y=0) sit under the top header and outside the 3:4 profile-grid crop. **Technical correctness**: there is no HDR→BT.709 tone-mapping (untreated iPhone HLG looks washed out, and the habitual gamma 1.18 is probably masking this). Mismatched clips get letterboxed, and Instagram demotes borders. The final mix's loudness is never measured. And the fade-to-black ending signals "swipe now". On the distribution side, the platform-confirmed levers are watch time, likes per reach and especially sends per reach, plus originality, a 3-minute ceiling, at most 5 hashtags, and Trial Reels for testing. So the skill should output a **publish package** next to the MP4: caption text, hashtags, alt text, a crop-safe cover and an upload checklist. Most of the precise numbers that circulate (skip-rate benchmarks, "interrupt every 5 s", caption-style uplift percentages, "-14 LUFS for Instagram") are vendor folklore, and this report labels them as such. Several changes are values decisions that belong to the user, not the pipeline: cutting religious greetings, speeding up or gap-trimming recitation, instrumental music, dropping a brand intro.

**Evidence key used throughout.** **[Platform]** means a statement, product change or documentation from Meta/Instagram, TikTok or YouTube. Many Mosseri statements reach us only through press, and this is flagged where it applies. **[Data]** means a third-party dataset with a stated sample. **[Academic]** means peer-reviewed research. **[Standard]** means broadcast, streaming or accessibility standards (Netflix, BBC, WCAG) or tool documentation. **[Folklore]** means vendor or creator heuristics with no disclosed method. **[Inference]** means the researchers' or this report's own reasoning, including pixel geometry computed from `build.mjs`.

## Viewers decide in three seconds; the skill shows its topic at eight

Instagram now measures exactly the window the skill currently spends on setup. **Reels Insights replaced "view rate" with Skip Rate, the share of first-time views that skip within the first 3 seconds, and added a per-second retention chart** [Platform] ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-adds-retention-insights-reels/758464/); [OnlineMarketing.de](https://onlinemarketing.de/social-media-marketing/instagram-neue-metriken-skiprate-sharerate-insights-reels)). Meta's December 2025 hook guidance defines the hook as "the text, visual, and audio components" of the first few seconds. It names three types: **value promise, statement of intent, and question/invitation** [Platform] ([Meta for Business](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/the-science-of-the-hook-how-to-supercharge-your-reels-performance)). Instagram publishes no "good" skip rate. Vendor thresholds converge on **skip below ~30% as healthy and above ~40% as a hook problem**, but these are [Folklore] ([Babbleboxx](https://www.babbleboxx.com/post/instagram-adds-reels-retention-skip-rate-what-influencer-marketers-should-do-next); [Retensis](https://retensis.com/blog/instagram-reels-skip-rate-benchmarks-2026)). The harder numbers point the same way. **Metricool puts the average Reel watch at about 8.5 s** [Data] ([Metricool](https://metricool.com/instagram-reels-guide/)). A 2024 ACM study of TikTok ads found **85.2% of viewers exit during the first 25% of the video** [Academic] ([Salminen et al. 2024](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3648188.3677048)).

Compare that with the skill's own example (`templates/project.example.json`). Segment 0 (raw 2.0–7.38 s, about 4.5–5 s after silence removal) is the hook. `introAfter: 0` then cuts to a full-screen intro clip. The title uses `after: "intro", offset: 0.4`, so it fades in roughly **8–10 s into the Reel**. By then the skip decision has been made and the average viewer has gone. The schema already supports the fix: `title: {t: 0, …}` places the card on the timeline at frame 0 (`at()` in `build.mjs`). The SKILL.md design default ("title … ~4.5 s after the intro") is the problem, not the code.

The intro clip is the second issue. **No platform has published data on intros or logo stings.** Every source found, vendor and creator alike, gives the same rule: never put an intro before the hook, and put branding inside the action or at the end [Folklore] ([Tiger Pistol](https://tigerpistol.com/vertical-video-ad-best-practices/); [Teleprompter.com](https://www.teleprompter.com/blog/youtube-audience-retention)). The skill already avoids the worst pattern. But a hard cut from the speaker to a separate branded clip is exactly what Renderforest describes as the dip where "the transition interrupt[s] momentum", and it recommends overlaying the branding instead [Folklore] ([Renderforest](https://www.renderforest.com/blog/intro-video-length-by-platform)). The numbers attached to this ("a 3-second logo is a 15% retention tax") are unsourced [Folklore] ([Gyre](https://gyre.pro/blog/how-to-make-a-youtube-intro-that-keeps-viewers-watching)). The direction is consistent, though, and the cost of a 3–4 s full-screen intro on a 45–90 s Reel is 4–9% of runtime with no information, placed at the steepest point of the retention curve [Inference]. The defensible default is **no intro clip**. Brand identity can come through a small corner bug, the title kicker, the caption text, or an end card of 1 s or less. If an organisation insists on a sting, it should be 1.5–2 s or shorter and ideally overlaid while the speaker's audio continues. The user can check its cost directly in the retention chart: a dip at the intro timestamp is the signal [Inference].

The hook should be **spoken and written**. Meta's own definition includes text, and it calls audio "the hidden gem", reporting up to 13% higher incremental conversions for Reels with music or voiceover. That figure comes from ads, not organic Reels [Platform] ([Meta for Business](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/the-science-of-the-hook-how-to-supercharge-your-reels-performance)). TikTok tells advertisers to introduce the proposition in the first 3 s and gives a text pace of 5–10 words per second [Platform] ([TikTok for Business](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/creative-best-practices)). Agency guidance adds ~5 words or fewer of large, uncluttered hook text [Folklore] ([AdStellar](https://www.adstellar.ai/blog/vertical-video-ad-best-practices)). For the skill this means a 3–6-word headline version of the spoken hook (the title, or a new `hook` field) visible from frame 0, while the speaker is already mid-sentence. The hook and the karaoke captions should not compete as two large texts in the first second. Either the first caption phrase is held until the hook text exits, or the hook text is a paraphrase rather than a duplicate [Inference].

The hook line itself is chosen during the script/take step (SKILL.md step 3), where the agent can apply rules that `lines.mjs` output makes easy. The first sentence must stand alone. Flag openings that start with a connective ("Und…", "Also…", "Çünkü…") or that point back to context ("dieser Punkt", "bu yüzden"). Faith-clip guidance says to start on "the sharpest line, not the setup" [Folklore] ([BlitzCut AI](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/faith-church-reel-hooks-2026)). The curiosity-gap literature backs hooks that point at a *specific* missing piece of something the audience half-knows ("Den zweiten Teil dieses Verses kennen die wenigsten"). It does not back hooks about something wholly unknown, or hooks that give the answer away. Loewenstein's work is [Academic] but was not tested on short video ([Golman & Loewenstein](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/golman/golman_loewenstein_curiosity.pdf)). Greetings and preamble ("Hallo zusammen", "Selam aleyküm kardeşlerim", "liebe Geschwister") are the most common reason a talking-head opening wastes its first seconds [Folklore] ([Inro](https://www.inro.social/guides/increase-instagram-reels-engagement)). For sermons, though, an opening salutation or Bismillah can be religiously or culturally obligatory. The skill should **propose** moving it to the end, the caption or a quiet tail, and **ask** rather than cut silently.

Endings and length close the loop. A visible outro (goodbye, fade to black, end screen) is widely reported to trigger the swipe and kill replays. This is [Folklore] with no platform quantification ([Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/p/youtube-shorts-low-watch-time-boost)). Instagram's watch time reportedly counts rewatches and both percentage and absolute seconds watched [Platform, secondhand] ([Fanpage Karma](https://www.fanpagekarma.com/insights/?p=9293)). The current `endFade: 0.8` is therefore the wrong default: a hard cut on the last word, or a last line that connects back to the hook, costs nothing and plausibly earns loops [Inference]. On length, Socialinsider's 2026 analysis of ~6M brand Reels found **45–60 s the best band for both engagement rate (0.35%) and median views (10,374)**. 30–90 s are all solid, and Reels over 3 minutes are not placed in the Reels tab [Data] ([Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/instagram-reels-length/)). Its reach-rate table peaks at **30–60 s (5.60%)** and falls to 3.50% above 120 s [Data] ([Socialinsider statistics](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/instagram-reels-statistics/)). Instagram raised the recommendable limit to 3 minutes in January 2025. Mosseri added "we still are focused on short form", and Instagram reportedly said Reels of 90 s or less "tend to perform better" [Platform] ([Lindsey Gamble](https://www.lindseygamble.com/blog/instagram-three-minute-reels); [Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/instagram-reels-tips-3-minutes-unfiltered-nyc/742335/)). The skill's typical 45–90 s output is in the sweet spot. `build.mjs` should still print a warning above 90 s and refuse above 180 s.

## Captions sit 150 px inside Meta's bottom dead zone

Meta publishes safe zones only for ads, but it publishes them clearly: **keep the top 14%, the bottom 35% and 6% on each side free of text**. At 1080×1920 that is 269 px top, 672 px bottom and 65 px sides, leaving a safe box of about 950×980 px between y≈269 and y≈1248 [Platform, ads] ([Meta Ads Guide](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads-guide/update/video/instagram-reels)). Nothing on Instagram's organic help pages gives an organic safe zone. Third-party screenshot measurements put the organic bottom overlay anywhere from 250 to 480 px, and the right action rail at roughly the rightmost 21% (~230 px) within the bottom ~40% of the frame [Folklore/measurement] ([Inro safe-zone checker](https://www.inro.social/tools/instagram-reels-safe-zone-checker); [Hopper HQ](https://www.hopperhq.com/blog/instagram-reel-size/)).

The skill's geometry, computed from `build.mjs` [Inference], looks like this. `.cap` sits at `bottom: 520px` with 64 px Inter 700 at `line-height: 1.42`, a line box of ~91 px. A one-line caption therefore spans y≈1309–1400, and a two-line one starts at y≈1218. That is clear of most organic measurements but **150 px below Meta's 35% line**. It also collides with long Instagram captions, the expanded "more" text, ads, and TikTok's commonly cited ~480 px bottom zone ([AdKit](https://adkit.so/tools/safe-zones/tiktok)). Moving `captions.bottom` to **~680 px** puts the block's bottom at y≈1240. Tightening `line-height` to ~1.2–1.25 keeps a two-line block's top near y≈1080, which in typical chest-up framing is still below the chin (snapshot-check the scrim overlap between lines). The width matters too. `S.capWidth: 920` centred spans x≈80–1000, which overlaps the rail zone (x≳850, y≳1150) on long lines. A width of ~840 px, optionally centred ~40 px left of frame centre, keeps text off the icons [Inference]. No single number is "correct". 680 px is the conservative cross-surface value, and ~600 px is a compromise if the speaker is framed so low that 680 px hits the mouth.

Caption length and timing need tightening. **Netflix allows 42 characters per line, 2 lines, 20 characters per second for adults (17 for children) and a minimum event of 5/6 s**. BBC guidance (secondhand) gives 160–180 wpm and ≈0.3 s per word [Standard] ([Netflix Timed Text Style Guide](https://partnerhelp.netflixstudios.com/hc/en-us/articles/217350977-English-Timed-Text-Style-Guide); [Clevercast summary of BBC](https://clevercast.com/bbc-subtitling-guidelines)). The skill's `maxChars: 28` is meant as one line, but at 64 px Inter Bold (≈0.55–0.6 em average advance) 28 characters plus scrim padding measure about 1,040–1,130 px. That wraps inside a 920 px box, so in practice the line breaks wherever the browser decides rather than at a phrase boundary [Inference]. The fix is a **22–24-character line target with an explicit, phrase-aware two-line break** (Netflix's bottom-heavy pyramid; never split article from noun). Because German compounds and Turkish agglutinative words make word counts meaningless, the character limit should govern for DE/TR. The minimum phrase duration in `capGroups` (`inT + 0.5`) is half of Netflix's floor. Raising it to **0.85–1.0 s** (merging shorter phrases into a neighbour) and printing a warning above 17 cps (failing above 20) catches the over-fast captions that a 1.15× speed-up produces [Standard → Inference].

Readability and tone favour the skill's restrained style. The scrim (`rgba(ink, .38)` on `.cap-inner`) is lighter than the 45–60% black the caption notes recommend. Raising it to ~0.5–0.55 guarantees WCAG's 4.5:1 contrast on bright backgrounds [Standard] ([W3C 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html)). The colour-only active-word highlight (80 ms in, 120 ms out) is the right call. The one controlled study found that time-synchronised keyword highlights helped learners but felt **"too intrusive" for ordinary viewing** [Academic, n=66] ([MUM 2024 via MCML](https://mcml.ai/publications/wwc+24/)). That argues against Hormozi-style pops for sermons. Claims that animated captions lift retention 15–20% have no published method, and even the vendor repeating them concedes no study compares the styles [Folklore] ([BlitzCut](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/animated-captions-vs-static-captions)). What the skill lacks is **semantic emphasis**: one theologically or factually key word per phrase at most, roughly one every 3–5 phrases. It would use the accent colour plus weight 800. That weight is set statically for the whole phrase, so it does not trigger the reflow the gotchas warn about. With emphasis in place, the running karaoke highlight could use a softer tone so gold keeps one meaning [Inference]. The caption motion (0.18 s fade with a 14 px rise on `power2.out`, 0.14 s exit) already matches Material's decelerate-in/accelerate-out convention and should stay. The one off-tone element is the ref chip's star, which spins in with `back.out(1.7)`. Overshoot reads as playful, so for sermons a plain `power2.out` fade is the better match [Inference].

The top of the frame has the same problem as the bottom. `S.titleTop: 210` and `S.refTop: 200` place the title card and source chips inside Meta's 269 px header zone, where the username, audio label and Reels header icons sit [Platform, ads] ([Hopper HQ](https://www.hopperhq.com/blog/instagram-reel-size/)). They also sit partly outside the profile grid. Mosseri announced the taller grid in January 2025 [Platform] ([onlinemarketing.de](https://onlinemarketing.de/social-media-marketing/instagram-vertical-statt-quadrat-im-grid)). The tile is a measured **3:4 crop that shows only y≈240–1680 of a 1920-tall frame** [Folklore/measurement] ([Inro](https://www.inro.social/tools/instagram-reels-safe-zone-checker)). A title top of **~310 px** and side padding of **72 px** (above Meta's 65 px) clear both. The serif title itself (Cormorant Garamond 700 at 96 px) is above the ≥600 weight and ≥64 px floor where its hairlines survive compression, so it can stay. The 8 px progress line at y=0 sits under the status bar and header and is cropped out of every grid and feed view. No evidence was found that progress bars help or hurt retention. It should be **off by default**, or moved to y≈276 at 3–4 px and ~50% opacity [Inference].

Two language details are easy to miss. The kicker uses `text-transform: uppercase`, which maps Turkish i→İ correctly **only when `<html lang="tr">` is set**. The schema defaults `lang` to `"en"`, so a Turkish project that forgets the field renders "ISTANBUL" instead of "İSTANBUL" [Inference]. `lang` should be required, or derived from the `transcribe.mjs --language` value, with a warning when they disagree. German quotes should be „…" (already handled via `captionFixes`). Religious terms and names are the most visible "AI caption" errors [Folklore] ([CapCut](https://www.capcut.com/create/auto-subtitles-vs-manual-captions)), so a reusable glossary file of spellings (Allah, Rahmân, surah names, speaker names) applied before `captionFixes` would remove the most repetitive manual work.

## The cut is already tight; the gaps are colour, loudness and framing

The silence parameters are close to mainstream. Auto-Editor, the most widely used open tool, defaults to **a 0.2 s margin on each side of speech** [Standard/tool default] ([Auto-Editor](https://github.com/wyattblue/auto-editor)). Descript shortens word gaps to a residual target (their example is 200 ms) rather than removing them [Standard/tool] ([Descript](https://help.descript.com/hc/en-us/articles/10164807277453-Shorten-word-gaps)). One reels-builder author notes that **margins below ~0.3 s "eat word edges"** [Folklore] ([tessl](https://tessl.io/registry/gamussa/reels-producer-skill/0.26.0/files/skills/reel-builder/scripts/tighten_vo.py)). The skill keeps `keepAfter: 0.10` plus `keepBefore: 0.08`, so each join has only **0.18 s** of handle. Moving to 0.15/0.10 (0.25 s per join) and raising `minGap` from 0.25 to 0.30 s keeps the "machine-gun" risk down without visibly slowing the cut [Inference]. `silences.mjs` uses a fixed `-35 dB`. Phone noise floors vary, and AutoCut moved to auto-detecting the threshold for that reason [Folklore] ([AutoCut](https://www.autocut.com/en/blogs/autocut-silences-parameters/)). The RMS helper already in `audit.mjs` can measure the 10th-percentile 50 ms level and set the threshold to noise floor +6–10 dB, clamped to −45…−30 dB [Inference]. `audit.mjs` should also gain QA checks: the first spoken word lands within 0.2 s of frame 0, no cut falls inside a word's timestamp span (allowing for Parakeet's known ~0.3 s early sentence starts), and no kept breath exceeds ~0.4 s.

Speed-up should follow measured speech rate, not habit. Lecture research found **no significant difference in attention, cognitive load or learning between 1.0× and 1.5×** [Academic] ([Yang et al. 2020](https://pure.ecnu.edu.cn/en/publications/time-compressed-audio-on-attention-meditation-cognitive-load-and-/)). Persuasion research across six studies (N=3,958) found faster speech first raises perceived confidence, then **the effect attenuates or reverses** [Academic] ([J Nonverbal Behav 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10919-024-00477-6)). Short-form wpm targets (140–200) are [Folklore] ([Teleprompter.com](https://www.teleprompter.com/tools/speaking-speed-calculator)). The skill's "Faster → `--speed 1.15`" recipe should become `speed: auto`. That means computing the articulation rate from word timestamps after silence removal, then setting speed = clamp(target/measured, 1.0, 1.15), with a target of ~150–160 wpm for reflective religious content and ~165 for explainers, and no speed-up if the speaker is already at or above target [Inference]. The bigger issue is **recitation**. Qur'an verses, scripture quotes and du'a have tempo and pauses that are part of correct recitation. They should be marked as protected spans that get neither atempo nor gap-trimming. Because `prep.mjs` bakes one global speed into `assets/main.mp4`, this needs per-segment speed, which is a larger change. Until then, the agent should **ask** before applying any speed to a recording that contains recitation.

Punch-ins are the main editing tool the skill lacks, and also the least evidenced. No retention data compares zoom frequencies or scales. Practitioner guidance favours a hard-cut punch landing exactly on an emphasised word's onset, one slow push reserved for the thesis line, and restraint, since "changing scale every few seconds on autopilot quickly becomes distracting" [Folklore] ([Tella](https://www.tella.com/skills/add-zooms); [faceless.so](https://faceless.so/blog/9-talking-head-video-tips-for-engaging-content)). Claimed cadence rules ("a new visual every 3–5 s") are vendor folklore ([Argil](https://argil.ai/blog/what-is-b-roll-the-complete-guide-for-video-creators-in-2026)). For serious content the defensible design is a **calm profile**: base 100%, punch 112–115%, at most one framing change per 3 s, on average one per 8–12 s, and one 100→106–108% push across a key sentence. The transform origin should keep the eyes in the upper third, and no punch should go above ~120%, because upscaling a 1080-wide proxy softens visibly [Inference]. In `build.mjs` this is a per-clip `gsap.set` scale on the `<video>` element at clip start, driven by script emphasis markers rather than a timer. It has to avoid the `gsap_css_transform_conflict` lint pitfall already noted in the gotchas.

The music-bed numbers are already sensible because `prep.mjs` loudness-normalises the music to −16 LUFS before any gain is applied. That is exactly the "loudness-match first" step the notes recommend. `music.bed: 0.12` therefore means a predictable **−18.4 dB** under speech. Accessibility guidance asks for background sound **at least 20 dB below speech** [Standard] ([WCAG 1.4.7](https://www.disabilityworld.org/toolkit/standards/wcag/1-4-7-low-or-no-background-audio/)), while a German public-broadcaster guideline accepts ≥7 LU [Standard] ([Salford](https://salford-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/1488623)). A default of **0.10 (−20 dB)**, with 0.06–0.08 for dense speech, is the safer middle [Inference]. The 2.5 s fade-out is long for a hard-cut ending; 1.0–1.5 s fits better. The music automation lane in `build.mjs` could also lift the bed slightly in pauses longer than ~1 s, which is easy because speech spans are known [Inference]. On music choice, Instagram deprioritises muted Reels [Platform] ([Instagram Ranking Explained](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained)). Business accounts are limited to Meta's Sound Collection, and licensed tracks can be muted later [Folklore/vendor] ([Soundstripe](https://www.soundstripe.com/blogs/instagram-music-library)). So baked-in beds must be royalty-free or self-licensed. Many Muslim audiences avoid instrumental music, so voice-only or vocal/drone beds should be a first-class option, and that choice belongs to the user.

Loudness is unspecified by every platform. **Neither Meta nor TikTok publishes a loudness target** [Platform, absence]. The common recommendation of −14 LUFS integrated with a true peak of −1 dBTP is [Folklore] ([OpenClip](https://openclip.app/learn/audio-normalization)). YouTube only turns loud content down. The skill's `LOUDNORM = "loudnorm=I=-16:TP=-1.5:LRA=11"` runs **one-pass** on each source. One-pass loudnorm runs in dynamic mode and can pump, whereas two-pass with measured values is accurate [Standard] ([FFmpeg filters](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html)). Nothing measures the final mixed render either. A `finalize.mjs` step should do two-pass loudnorm on the rendered MP4's audio (video stream copied). Its default target would be −14 LUFS / −1 dBTP, configurable back to −16, and it should write a QA line with integrated LUFS, true peak, duration and colour tags. An optional `--clean` voice chain in `prep.mjs` would fix most phone recordings without the "muffled and watery" result of aggressive denoise [Folklore/practitioner] ([pyvideotrans](https://en.pyvideotrans.com/blog/ffmpeg-nosie)): `highpass=f=80`, `afftdn` at nf≈−25 chosen from the measured noise floor, a gentle de-esser, and light compression. It should also be applied to `edit/main.wav` so silence detection runs on cleaned audio.

The most consequential technical fix is colour. **iPhones record HLG HDR (BT.2020, arib-std-b67) by default. A naive scale/format conversion "produces a washed-out, gray image"** unless it is tone-mapped through `zscale`+`tonemap` or `libplacebo` [Standard/tool docs] ([video-use HDR handling](https://instagit.com/browser-use/video-use/how-does-video-use-handle-hdr-hlg-pq-source-footage-from-devices-like-iphones/)). Meta's own engineers note that Instagram generates SDR versions of HDR Reels and tone-mapping can shift colour and brightness [Platform] ([Engineering at Meta](https://engineering.fb.com/2023/07/17/video-engineering/hdr-video-reels-meta/)). `prep.mjs`'s `probe()` reads only `codec_type`, size, frame rate and rotation, and `fit()` only scales and pads. The gotchas confirm the inputs are iPhone HEVC 1728×3072. So the skill's standing advice that "gamma ~1.15–1.2 lifts faces" is very plausibly compensating for untone-mapped HLG [Inference]. `probe()` should read `color_transfer`/`color_primaries`/`pix_fmt`. If the transfer is HLG or PQ, `prep.mjs` should tone-map to BT.709 before any grade (and warn if the ffmpeg build lacks zimg). The output should be tagged `-colorspace bt709 -color_primaries bt709 -color_trc bt709 -color_range tv`. Then `grade-test.mjs` candidates should be re-baselined, where gamma will probably drop to ~1.0–1.08.

`fit()` has a second flaw: `force_original_aspect_ratio=decrease,pad=…` **letterboxes** any clip whose aspect doesn't match the canvas, such as a 16:9 intro on a portrait Reel. Instagram explicitly says it shows fewer Reels with **borders**, along with low-resolution, watermarked, muted or mostly-text Reels [Platform] ([Instagram Ranking Explained](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained); [Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2021/02/10/instagram-reels-with-tiktok-watermark-less-discoverable/)). The default should be cover-crop (`force_original_aspect_ratio=increase,crop=W:H`), with a printed warning whenever padding would have been needed. Related to this, `canvasFor()` turns a landscape main recording into a 1920×1080 output. For a skill that targets Reels, a landscape sermon recording should be reframed to 9:16 instead, ideally around the speaker's face. The remaining export settings already match Meta's spec (H.264, square pixels, fixed frame rate, stereo AAC ≥128 kbps) [Platform] ([Meta Ads Guide](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads-guide/update/video/instagram-reels)). The 1-second GOP in `VIDEO_OUT` is a renderer-seeking requirement for the proxy (see gotchas), not a delivery setting, so it should stay.

## Distribution rewards sends, originality and a clean native upload

Instagram's officially named Reels signals are **watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach**. Mosseri said **sends weigh slightly more for non-followers** and likes slightly more for followers [Platform, via press] ([Search Engine Journal](https://searchenginejournal.com/instagram-algorithm-shift-why-sends-matter-more-than-ever/521389); [Dataslayer](https://www.dataslayer.ai/blog/instagram-algorithm-2025-complete-guide-for-marketers)). Since April 2024, qualifying content is first shown to a small audience "regardless of whether those users follow the account" and then expanded if it performs [Platform] ([Instagram Creators](https://creators.instagram.com/recommendations-and-originality)). Mosseri's September 2026 overview names **originality, recency, "breaking" small creators to non-followers, and larger ranking models** as the four principles, and tells creators to watch engagement rate rather than raw views [Platform] ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-chief-shares-algorithm-insights-and-posting-tips/831326/)). The popular claim that "a send is worth 3–5 likes" has no official source [Folklore] ([Creator Lane](https://creatorlanehq.com/blog/instagram-algorithm-2026-mosseri-vs-measured)). The practical consequence for the skill is that every ratio is judged on the first test audience. **The first three seconds and the burned-in captions matter more than anything done at upload**, and the best CTA for sermon content is a specific *send* prompt ("Schick das jemandem, der das heute hören muss" / "Bunu duyması gereken birine gönder") rather than "like and comment" [Inference]. Instagram's Recommendation Guidelines exclude **"clickbait or engagement bait"**, so "comment Amin" prompts are a reach risk. They also exclude **political content about laws, elections or social issues**, so the package should flag sermons that touch such topics and warn the user to expect reach mainly among followers [Platform] ([Instagram Creators](https://creators.instagram.com/recommendations-and-originality)).

Originality and upload hygiene are now enforced. Accounts that post others' content without material edits **10+ times in 30 days** lose recommendation eligibility. When duplicates exist, only the original is recommended. Watermarks are disqualifying, and the April 2026 extension states that watermarks and credits are not "material edits" [Platform] ([Instagram Creators](https://creators.instagram.com/recommendations-and-originality); [Engadget](https://engadget.com/2160560/instagrams-recommendation-algorithm-will-penalize-unoriginal-photo-and-carousel-posts)). A speaker filming their own talking head is clearly original. The real risks for this user are two accounts (the mosque and the speaker) uploading the same file, and clips re-downloaded from TikTok. **Collab posts** are Instagram's own mechanism for one original with two audiences [Platform] ([Engadget](https://engadget.com/2160560/instagrams-recommendation-algorithm-will-penalize-unoriginal-photo-and-carousel-posts)). **Trial Reels**, launched December 2024, show a Reel only to non-followers first, report results after ~24 h, and can auto-share to followers based on 72 h performance. Mosseri recommended them for experimentation in September 2026 [Platform] ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/technology/news/story/instagram-launches-trial-reels-letting-creators-test-content-before-reaching-followers-457022-2024-12-12); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-chief-shares-algorithm-insights-and-posting-tips/831326/)). They fit hook A/B tests of evergreen sermon clips. They are wrong for time-sensitive announcements (Jumu'ah topics, Ramadan or Bayram times), where followers are the audience.

The caption text is the last lever the skill doesn't touch. Instagram moved to a **maximum of 5 hashtags per post in December 2025**, framing hashtags as categorisation [Platform, via press] ([Later](https://later.com/blog/instagram-hashtags/amp); [GIGA](https://www.giga.de/tech/instagram-nur-noch-3-5-hashtags-so-geht-ihr-mit-dem-limit-um--01J5QKD19082YNPRSGQTN7ESTT)). Third-party reports say public professional-account posts have been indexed by Google since about July 2025, including captions and alt text [Folklore/3P reports] ([ContentGrip](https://www.contentgrip.com/instagram-searchable-on-google/)). Advice to front-load keywords in the first line is [Folklore] ([Kontentino](https://www.kontentino.com/q-and-a/instagram-search-seo/)), but it costs nothing. Whether on-screen text or speech is indexed is unknown. Burned-in captions overlap with Instagram's auto-generated captions, "making it hard to read either set" (RNID, 2022), so the upload checklist must say to switch auto-captions off [Folklore/older] ([RNID](https://rnid.org.uk/information-and-support/technology-and-products/how-to-use-video-captions-subtitles-social-media-accessibility/instagram/)). **Meta AI Reels translation added German on 14 July 2026; Turkish is not supported** [Platform] ([Meta newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/10/discover-reels-around-world-meta-ai-translation/)). A German Reel with burned-in German captions will show those captions over dubbed audio, so the user should choose consciously. On timing, Mosseri says there is no ideal posting frequency and recency matters [Platform] ([onlinemarketing.de](https://onlinemarketing.de/social-media-marketing/instagrams-posting-tipp-frequenz-wie-lang-pause)). Third-party "best times" conflict: Buffer's 9.6M posts favour midweek midday and evening, while Later favours ~5 am [Data] ([Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/when-is-the-best-time-to-post-on-instagram)). The package should therefore suggest a starting slot, such as midweek early evening local time or Thursday evening before Jumu'ah (an inference with no data behind it), and tell the user to iterate from their own Insights.

For faith content specifically there is little independent evidence. One church's two best posts in 120 days were sermon Reels with ~3,000 shares combined [Folklore/anecdotal] ([Pro Church Tools](https://167.prochurchtools.com/p/sermon-reels-are-finally-dead)). A Baltimore mosque's everyday, humanising clips drew one imam clip past 16M views [press case] ([Religion News Service](https://religionnews.com/2026/03/25/were-not-strangers-how-a-baltimore-masjids-relatable-videos-led-to-instagram-fame/)). Arousal research suggests awe and uplift are shared more than low-arousal sadness, though it was derived from news articles, not video [Academic] ([Berger & Milkman](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2011/11/Virality.pdf)). Together these support **one sendable idea per Reel**, with the topic named explicitly in the first sentence, the hook text and the caption.

## A publish package beside every MP4

A new `scripts/package.mjs` (run after render) should write `out/<name>.publish.md` and `out/<name>.cover.png` from data the skill already has: `project.json` (title, kicker, lang, speaker in the subtitle), `timeline.json` (duration, caption text = full transcript), and the finalize QA numbers. The agent drafts the caption and hashtags and the user approves them. The cover should be a dedicated 1080×1920 render (a `build.mjs --cover <t>` variant: one face frame, no captions, title centred) with the face and a 2–5-word title inside **y≈420–1500**, the band that survives the 3:4 grid, 4:5 and 1:1 crops [Inference from crop maths] ([Hopper HQ](https://www.hopperhq.com/blog/instagram-reel-size/)). ## Decisions the skill must hand back to the user

| Decision | Default the skill proposes | Why it needs the user |
|---|---|---|
| Opening greeting / Bismillah / salutation | Move out of seconds 0–3 (to the end, the caption or a quiet tail) | Religious and cultural obligation outweighs retention folklore |
| Speed-up and gap-trimming on Qur'an recitation, scripture or du'a | Protect: no atempo, no silence cuts | Tempo and pauses are part of correct recitation |
| Instrumental music bed | Offer voice-only or vocal/drone beds alongside instrumental | Many Muslim audiences avoid instruments |
| Brand intro clip | None; corner bug + kicker + ≤1 s end card | Organisation branding is the owner's call; the retention chart can settle it |
| Figurative AI imagery or B-roll for spiritual statements | Typographic verse/quote cards only | Aniconism and tone sensitivity |
| CTA wording | One send- or save-type prompt | Some congregations dislike "share" asks; engagement bait is a reach risk |
| Political or social-issue sermons | Flag in package | Not recommendable per Instagram guidelines, so reach mostly limited to followers |
| Trial Reels / AI dubbing / Collab partner | Suggest per Reel | Audience, timing and account relationships are the user's |

## Conclusion and prioritised change list

The research shifts where the skill's effort should go. Its current strengths are in the *edit*: which words survive and how tight the joins are. Platform evidence says the distribution outcome is decided in the *first three seconds and in the frame layout*, then in a clean, original upload with one sendable idea. The cheapest high-value changes are therefore **default values and SKILL.md instructions, not new code**. Moving the title to frame 0, dropping the default intro, raising the caption band, removing the fade-out and lowering the progress line are all one-line changes in `build.mjs` or the example project. The one genuinely corrective engineering task is HDR tone-mapping: if the source is HLG, every Reel so far has been graded on a washed-out base, and that likely explains why "a bit brighter" became a standing request. Evidence is weakest exactly where vendors are loudest (caption animation styles, interrupt cadence, loudness targets), so the skill should treat those values as tunable defaults and close the loop with the user's own Skip Rate and retention chart rather than chasing published benchmarks.
